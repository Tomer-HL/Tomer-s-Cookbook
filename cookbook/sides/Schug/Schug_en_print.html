Schug (Yemenite Hot Sauce)

Schug is Israel's national hot sauce — brought over by Yemenite Jews and embraced across the country ever since. It's a bold, garlicky, flavor-packed condiment that goes well with just about everything. The name comes from the maschag, a traditional grinding stone used to break down raw peppers and garlic into a paste.

Ingredients

  • 12 hot peppers, red or green (small and thin variety)
  • 1 head of garlic, peeled
  • 1 bunch of cilantro
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/4–1/2 cup water
  • 1 tsp cardamom (optional)

Instructions

  1. Cut the stems off the peppers, but leave the seeds in — that's where the heat is. For best results, use small thin hot peppers, usually found at a Latin or Middle Eastern grocery.
  2. Roughly chop the peppers and add to a food processor together with the garlic. Pulse for about 30 seconds.
  3. Roughly chop the cilantro and add to the food processor along with the cumin, salt, cardamom, and water — start with 1/4 cup and add more if needed. Pulse for another 30 seconds until you reach a coarse, spreadable texture.
  4. Transfer to a clean, sealed jar and refrigerate. Keeps for up to 2 weeks in the fridge.
    Tip: Freeze portions in small Ziploc bags and transfer to the fridge when ready to use.
    Yield: Approximately 1–1.5 cups
